# Sattva Labs

## New ways to describe, explore, and compare AI cognitive configurations

I’m Amaia Amezaga, an independent researcher working across AI evaluation, human-AI interaction, AI ethics, sociotechnical AI safety, and public understanding of AI.

I founded Sattva Labs to investigate how we can understand and orient artificial intelligence systems whose cognitive organisation cannot be assumed in advance. AI systems are not human minds. At the same time, descriptions tied too closely to particular models, architectures, tasks, or benchmarks can quickly become outdated.

Sattva Labs explores dynamic and multidimensional ways of describing artificial intelligence that can remain useful as technologies change. Its work distinguishes between capacities that are often treated as equivalent. Language fluency, memory, analytical ability, persuasive reasoning, judgment, understanding, wisdom, and ethical discernment may interact, but one does not necessarily imply another.

## What I investigate

Most descriptions of AI systems tell us how well a model performs on a particular task or use broad labels such as reasoning, intelligence, memory, or agency. These descriptions can be useful, but they do not always show how different capabilities, limitations, and behavioural tendencies coexist within the same system.

A model may be highly fluent but unreliable, persuasive without sound judgment, capable in one context and unstable in another. The combination of these characteristics shapes how people interpret the system, what they expect from it, and how much responsibility they are willing to delegate.

Sattva Labs investigates whether multidimensional cognitive representations can show:

- which capabilities and limitations coexist;
- how different dimensions interact or constrain one another;
- where confidence and observed reliability diverge;
- how configurations vary across contexts and over time;
- and how interaction with a system affects human interpretation and reliance.

The aim is to develop a visual and conceptual language for comparing AI systems without treating human cognition as the default template. This work also asks whether evaluation can be organised around recurring patterns of behaviour and interaction rather than particular model versions, architectures, or short-lived benchmarks.

## Why this matters for AI safety

People do not decide whether to trust an AI system from benchmark results alone. They form an impression through its language, apparent coherence, interface, previous successes, and behaviour across repeated interactions.

When visible strengths obscure less visible limitations, users may attribute understanding, judgment, or reliability that the system has not demonstrated. This can lead to poorly calibrated reliance and to the delegation of decisions, interpretation, or evaluation on the basis of an incomplete model of the system’s capabilities.

The problem becomes more serious when the system also participates in shaping the user’s reasoning. Sycophancy, persuasion, cognitive offloading, and repeated reinforcement can make an initial misunderstanding harder to detect from within the interaction. Meaningful human oversight therefore depends partly on making a system’s uneven capabilities, limitations, and influence easier to recognise.

Sattva Labs treats communication as part of this safety problem. Performance claims, warnings, and isolated explanations of model limitations may provide information without giving people useful ways to interpret a system’s behaviour. Conceptual and visual representations can offer mental tools for distinguishing fluency from understanding, confidence from reliability, assistance from influence, and successful performance from sound judgment.

A shared language may also help researchers, designers, policymakers, domain experts, and users connect evidence that is currently fragmented across disciplines. Building these connections is part of supporting human autonomy and effective sociotechnical oversight.

## Current project: Cognitive Topology Project

The Cognitive Topology Project is the first research project developed through Sattva Labs. It explores a new visual and conceptual language for describing AI systems as multidimensional, dynamic configurations of capabilities, limitations, and behavioural tendencies.

The project focuses on how different dimensions relate to one another: which characteristics coexist, reinforce or limit each other, and form a particular configuration under specific conditions. The term *topology* refers to this relational structure. Fluency, for example, means something different when combined with reliable reasoning than when combined with high confidence, instability, sycophancy, or poor recognition of uncertainty.

## Research approach

Sattva Labs combines conceptual research, qualitative AI evaluation, Human-Computer Interaction, AI ethics, non-western architectures of cognition, semantics, and Research through Design. These approaches are used together because sociotechnical AI safety cannot be understood by examining model outputs, technical systems, or human behaviour in isolation.

### Conceptual research

Conceptual research is treated as a scientific contribution in its own right. The categories used to define a problem shape what can be observed, compared, measured, and communicated.

### Research direction

The project currently explores:

- which dimensions are needed to describe AI cognitive and behavioural configurations;
- how capabilities, limitations, uncertainty, and behavioural tendencies relate to one another;
- how configurations vary across contexts, interactions, and time;
- how system-level characteristics can be distinguished from effects that emerge through human-AI interaction;
- how profiles can be compared without using human cognition as the default standard;
- and how visual and interactive representations affect interpretation, expectation, and reliance.

Cognitive topology organises and interprets evidence about observable behaviour, functional capacities, limitations, and relational effects. It does not claim direct access to a model’s internal experience or offer a definitive map of how it “thinks.”

### Research through representation

Visualisation is used as a method of inquiry, not only as a way of presenting finished findings.

Translating a concept into spatial, visual, or interactive form requires decisions about dimensions, relationships, boundaries, uncertainty, evidence, and change. Making those decisions explicit can reveal hidden assumptions, conceptual gaps, and contradictions that are less visible in prose or numerical scores.

The project will develop representations at different levels of complexity, from expandable web-based profiles to spatial and potentially immersive models. These prototypes will be examined with researchers, practitioners, domain experts, and non-specialist users to test which distinctions are meaningful, where a representation clarifies AI behaviour, and where it creates new misconceptions.

### Behavioural and relational evaluation

Sattva Labs focuses on observable patterns rather than making claims about a system’s internal experience.

Evaluation may examine how a system responds to uncertainty, conflicting instructions, ambiguity, correction, persuasion, changing context, and prolonged interaction. It also considers effects that arise through interface design, framing, interaction history, and user expectations.

The purpose is not merely to record whether a system succeeds or fails at a task, but to identify recurring configurations of capability, limitation, confidence, instability, and influence.

### Evaluation across technological change

Sattva Labs seeks to separate the evaluation framework from the evidence used to assess a specific system.

The dimensions used to describe a system should not depend on a single benchmark, model version, architecture, or current idea of what AI can do. This means testing the framework across different systems and contexts, identifying where it does not work, and revising it when necessary. Technological change may require new evidence and adjustments, but not an entirely new conceptual framework each time.

### Epistemic limits

The resulting frameworks and representations are provisional. Their value depends on the quality and diversity of the evidence used, the transparency of the interpretive choices involved, and their capacity to be challenged and revised.

Sattva Labs therefore aims to make uncertainty, evidential limits, and changes in assessment visible rather than presenting cognitive profiles as complete or permanent descriptions.

### Current stage

The project is currently in its conceptual research and early design phase. Its immediate priorities are to define an initial set of dimensions, establish a transparent basis for evaluating them, and develop and test low-fidelity representations with different audiences.

## About Amaia Amezaga

Sattva Labs was founded by Amaia Amezaga, an independent researcher and multilingual AI evaluator. Her background brings together interaction design, language, qualitative evaluation, and philosophy. She has completed AI safety training through BlueDot Impact and conducts independent adversarial experiments.

Since 2025, she has conducted professional qualitative evaluations of LLM behaviour, including instruction following, reasoning quality, hallucinations, ambiguity handling, and recurrent failure patterns. This sustained engagement grounds her independent research in direct observation across varied interactions and evaluation contexts.

In 2026, she contributed to the Apart Research Global South AI Safety Hackathon as a speaker and judge. Her talk examined the sociotechnical risks of conversational AI, the need to preserve human judgment and autonomy, and the importance of broader public participation in shaping how these systems are integrated into society. It was intentionally framed in accessible language to connect specialist debates with the lived experience of AI users.
